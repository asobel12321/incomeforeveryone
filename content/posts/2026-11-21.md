AI is no longer just a back-office efficiency story in labor markets; it is changing how people apply, how employers screen, and how public systems prepare for displacement. Today’s evidence shows three different responses: recruitment automation in the EU, retraining support for laid-off workers in Michigan, and fresh UK research on what actually helps people move back into work.

---

### Key Stories

- **EU recruiters add anti-fraud checks as AI use rises**
  EURES says jobseekers are using AI to draft applications while employers are using chatbots for screening and scheduling, which is pushing recruiters to add identity checks and skills tests. That matters for UBI and labor policy because AI is not only displacing tasks inside firms; it is also changing the friction and fairness of getting hired in the first place.
  [Chatbots are changing how we look for jobs and talent. Are you ready?](https://eures.europa.eu/chatbots-are-changing-how-we-look-jobs-and-talent-are-you-ready-2026-11-20_en)

- **Michigan funds retraining for dislocated workers**
  Michigan Works! West Central said on November 18 that it secured a $6.6 million federal grant for the state’s Rapid Reskill Initiative, with employer-driven training, apprenticeships, and AI-enabled career-navigation tools. The concrete policy signal is that workforce systems are planning for layoffs and restructuring by speeding retraining rather than assuming workers can absorb the shock on their own.
  [6.6 Million To Help Dislocated Workers Train For In-Demand Jobs](https://www.mwwc.org/6-6-million-to-help-dislocated-workers-train-for-in-demand-jobs)

- **UK research tests what retraining actually delivers**
  The UK Department for Work and Pensions released a technical report on November 17 reviewing local retraining, upskilling, apprenticeships, and job-search support. For the AI and UBI debate, the important point is not simply that retraining exists, but that governments are still trying to identify which interventions move people into work and which only delay income loss.
  [What works in supporting people to retrain at the local level: technical report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/what-works-in-supporting-people-to-retrain-at-the-local-level/what-works-in-supporting-people-to-retrain-at-the-local-level-technical-report)

### What This Tells Us

Today’s evidence points to a labor market adjustment phase, not a single layoff shock: AI is changing hiring behavior, public agencies are funding reskilling, and policymakers are still searching for the most effective way to protect incomes when workers are displaced. That makes the UBI question more practical than theoretical—less about distant automation scenarios and more about whether income support can bridge the gap while hiring systems, training pipelines, and screening tools all get rewritten at once.
