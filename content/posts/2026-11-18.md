New evidence today points to a labor-market risk that is easy to miss if you only look for headline layoffs: AI exposure appears to be reducing worker mobility, just as vacancy growth is weakening in the U.K. That combination matters because slower reemployment can make restructuring and displacement more painful, even before unemployment surges.

---

### Key Stories

- **AI exposure is hurting job-finding and job-switching**
  A new San Francisco Fed paper says workers with higher AI exposure and adoption have seen larger declines in job-finding and job-switching rates, with a wider spread in prospects after the LLM era began. For labor policy, that shifts the UBI conversation from abstract job-loss fears to a more immediate problem: how workers stay afloat when reentry into work gets harder.
  [Artificial Intelligence and Labor Market Reallocation](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/board-of-governors/2026/11/artificial-intelligence-labor-market-reallocation/)

- **U.K. vacancies keep falling in November data**
  The U.K. Office for National Statistics said on Nov. 17 that vacancies fell further in its November 2026 labour market overview, even as employment rose and unemployment eased. A thinner openings market leaves less slack for workers displaced by automation or restructuring to quickly land somewhere else.
  [Labour market overview, UK: November 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/bulletins/uklabourmarket/november2026)

- **EU skills policy is focusing on portability**
  The European Commission’s Nov. 12 notice on a Pact for Skills–EAfA webinar centers on cross-border labour mobility and skills portability, including recognition of qualifications and reskilling barriers. That is a practical response to AI-era churn: if jobs and tasks change faster than credentials, workers need smoother transitions, not just more training slogans.
  [Register for joint Pact for Skills–EAfA webinar on cross-border labour mobility and skills portability](https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/register-joint-pact-skills-eafa-webinar-cross-border-labour-mobility-and-skills-portability-2026-11-12_en)

### What This Tells Us

Today’s evidence suggests AI’s labor impact is showing up first in worker mobility rather than in mass layoffs. If openings keep thinning while exposed workers take longer to find new jobs, the policy case for income support becomes less about cushioning a rare shock and more about bridging a more persistent transition.
