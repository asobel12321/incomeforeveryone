The newest evidence this week is not a layoff wave, but a policy-backed snapshot of where AI is already changing work. The European Commission says roughly 30% of EU workers now use AI in daily work, and that gap between exposure and adoption is shaping how governments, employers, and labor-market institutions respond.

---

### Key Stories

- **EU says AI is already part of daily work for 30%**
  The Commission’s Oct. 23 briefing is the clearest fresh signal on labor exposure today: AI is no longer confined to frontier firms, but it is still unevenly adopted, especially in smaller companies and among mid-career workers. That points to a near-term risk of task reshaping and wage pressure more than immediate mass displacement, and it strengthens the case for retraining or income-support backstops like UBI if transitions speed up.
  [AI and skills in Europe – Closing the adoption gap](https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/ai-and-skills-europe-closing-adoption-gap-2026-10-23_en?prefLang=lv)

- **Public employment services are adopting AI too**
  The Commission said on Oct. 22 that Europe’s public employment services are modernizing with digital tools and AI to improve job matching, training, and benefit support. That matters for labor displacement because it shows governments preparing the safety net and placement machinery for more churn in the job market, rather than waiting for layoffs to show up first.
  [Taking the European Network of Public Employment Services forward](https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/taking-european-network-public-employment-services-forward-2026-10-22_en?prefLang=lt)

- **Microsoft says Volkswagen rolled Copilot out to 330,000 employees**
  Microsoft’s Oct. 21 announcement says Volkswagen Group has now deployed Copilot to more than 330,000 workers, with more than 26,000 agents active across the company. The scale matters: it is evidence that AI is moving into core operations across engineering, production, procurement, and sales, which increases the odds of workforce restructuring even when headcount is not cut immediately.
  [Microsoft empowers more than 330,000 Volkswagen Group employees with Copilot](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2026/10/21/microsoft-empowers-more-than-330000-volkswagen-group-employees-with-copilot)

### What This Tells Us

Today’s evidence points to a labor transition centered on adoption, not just layoffs: AI is now widespread enough to affect routine work, public agencies are building matching and support systems around it, and large employers are scaling deployment across whole workforces. That combination raises the risk of job redesign and selective displacement, while also making stronger worker-transition policy more urgent if the benefits are not to accrue faster than the protections.
