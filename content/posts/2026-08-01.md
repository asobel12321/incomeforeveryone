AI is no longer just a future labor-market risk; it is increasingly showing up in real restructuring decisions, layoffs, and role eliminations across major companies. This week’s news suggests firms are using AI both to justify cuts and to reorganize work around smaller, more automated teams.

---

### Key Stories

- **Microsoft trims 4,800 jobs while shifting more resources toward AI**
  Microsoft said it was cutting about 2.1% of its workforce, or roughly 4,800 jobs, as it restructures parts of its commercial and Xbox businesses. The move is a clear example of how AI-era investment priorities are reshaping headcount even when companies frame the cuts as operational realignment rather than direct automation.  
  [Microsoft to cut 4,800 jobs, overhaul Xbox unit](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/microsoft-joins-aidriven-tech-layoff-wave-with-4800-job-cuts-4776805)

- **Amazon cuts jobs in its artificial general intelligence group**
  Amazon cut jobs inside its AGI group, continuing a broader pattern of smaller reductions across the company since January. When layoffs land directly inside an AI unit, it reinforces the labor-market paradox: companies are expanding AI capabilities while simultaneously shrinking parts of the workforce that build and support them.  
  [Amazon cuts jobs in its artificial general intelligence group](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/amazon-cuts-jobs-in-its-artificial-general-intelligence-group-4806431)

- **Meta layoffs face legal scrutiny over AI-powered selection tools**
  A U.S. judge declined to block Meta from laying off workers who allege they were selected with help from AI-powered tools, including claims tied to disability and medical leave. That raises an important UBI-era concern: if automation does not just replace jobs but also shapes who loses them, workers need stronger income-floor protections and clearer rules around algorithmic management.  
  [US judge won’t block Meta from laying off workers who filed AI discrimination lawsuit](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/us-judge-wont-block-meta-from-laying-off-workers-who-filed-ai-discrimination-lawsuit-4798876)

---

### What This Tells Us

The latest round of tech cutbacks shows that AI is no longer only a productivity story; it is becoming a restructuring tool that can reduce labor demand, intensify job-market insecurity, and shift risk onto workers. If this pattern continues, the case for a Universal Basic Income grows stronger as a practical stabilizer for households facing recurring disruption, not just one-time layoffs.
