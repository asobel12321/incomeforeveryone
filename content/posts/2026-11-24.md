The newest evidence today is less about a single layoff shock than about how quickly employers and statisticians are trying to catch up with labor-market change. In the UK, the ONS has published fresh job-flow measures, while separate research says most companies still have not fully built AI into workforce planning.

---

### Key Stories

- **ONS publishes fresh UK job-flow measures**
  The ONS released new LEED-based data on hires, separations, and job-to-job moves on November 23, giving a current view of whether workers are shifting roles more rapidly or getting stuck in place. That matters for AI and automation because tighter job flows can make displacement harder to absorb, and can strengthen the case for income support if reemployment slows.
  [New insights on labour market dynamics from UK linked employer-employee data (LEED): 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/articles/newinsightsonlabourmarketdynamicsfromuklinkedemployeremployeedataleed/2026)

- **ONS updates its labor-market measurement overhaul**
  The ONS also released a November 23 progress update on its labor-market transformation work, including the Transformed Labour Force Survey and use of HMRC PAYE RTI data to reduce bias from non-response. For AI and layoff tracking, this is a reminder that the quality of labor data itself is becoming part of the policy challenge: weaker measurement makes it harder to spot restructuring early.
  [Labour market transformation – update on progress and plans: November 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/articles/labourmarkettransformationupdateonprogressandplans/november2026)

- **Most firms still have not baked AI into workforce plans**
  The Conference Board says only 11% of companies have fully integrated AI implications into workforce and financial planning, even though many expect job redesign and upskilling. That gap suggests employers may still be moving faster on AI adoption than on the wage, retraining, and job-loss planning that would matter if displacement accelerates.
  [11% of Companies Have Fully Integrated AI Implications into Workforce Planning](https://www.conference-board.org/press/ai-implications-workforce-planning)

---

### What This Tells Us

Today's evidence points to a labor market that is being monitored more closely than it is being stabilized. The ONS data help show how workers are moving, while the Conference Board finding shows many employers still have not translated AI risk into concrete staffing plans—exactly the kind of gap that makes retraining and income-support policy more relevant if job churn picks up.
