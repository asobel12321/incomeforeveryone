AI-related job cuts are no longer a theoretical warning—they are showing up in real restructuring decisions, legal disputes, and fresh calls for policy action. Today’s stories point to a labor market where automation is changing how firms organize work faster than public safety nets are adapting.

---

### Key Stories

- **Microsoft’s 4,800-job cut shows AI spending is reshaping white-collar work**
  Microsoft said on July 6 it would cut about 2.1% of its workforce as it restructures parts of its commercial and Xbox businesses while shifting more capital toward AI infrastructure. For labor markets, that’s a clear sign that automation-era investment can coincide with headcount reduction even before AI fully replaces tasks.  
  [Microsoft joins AI-driven tech layoff wave with 4,800 job cuts](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/microsoft-joins-ai-driven-tech-layoff-wave-with-4800-job-cuts-2026-07-06/)

- **Thomson Reuters trims engineering roles as AI makes coding more efficient**
  Thomson Reuters said in July it would cut a small number of engineering jobs, with reporting tying the move to AI tools making software development more efficient. That matters for UBI and workforce policy because it shows displacement pressure reaching high-skill knowledge work, not just routine labor.  
  [Thomson Reuters to cut 'small number' of engineering jobs](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/thomson-reuters-cut-small-number-engineering-jobs-2026-07-13/)

- **More than 200 economists and researchers are urging governments to act now on AI job displacement**
  Reuters reported on July 13 that over 200 experts, including Nobel laureates and AI researchers, called for urgent policy institutions to address AI’s economic impact and the risk of large-scale job displacement. That kind of warning strengthens the case for UBI-style income floors as one part of a broader response to automation shocks.  
  [Over 200 experts call for urgent action to tackle AI’s economic impact](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/over-200-experts-call-urgent-action-tackle-ais-economic-impact-2026-07-13/)

---

### What This Tells Us

The pattern is getting harder to ignore: companies are using AI not only to automate tasks, but also to justify restructuring and smaller workforces. At the same time, credible experts are saying the policy response needs to be built now—before displacement spreads further—making UBI and related income-support ideas increasingly central to the debate.
