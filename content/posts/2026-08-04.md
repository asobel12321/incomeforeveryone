AI is no longer just a productivity story; it is now showing up in layoffs, job redesign, and fresh debate over how much labor disruption governments and employers should expect. Today’s snapshot pulls together three current signals: a live BLS reading on labor churn, a wave of company restructuring tied to AI, and new research that explicitly tests Universal Basic Income as a response.

---

### Key Stories

- **BLS releases new labor-turnover data as employers keep churning staff**
  The Bureau of Labor Statistics is releasing June 2026 JOLTS data today, and its latest published figures show May job openings at 7.594 million with layoffs/discharges at a 1.1% rate. For AI and UBI watchers, the key issue is whether a still-healthy labor market can absorb restructuring shocks before automation-driven displacement becomes broader and more persistent.  
  [Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey for June 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.htm)

- **Companies are still pointing to AI when cutting jobs**
  AP’s recent reporting shows how firms from tech to industrials are folding AI and automation into layoff announcements, even when they also cite restructuring or cost-cutting. That pattern matters for labor-market risk: AI is increasingly being used not only to automate tasks, but also to justify organizational redesign that can reduce headcount.  
  [From Cisco to Block, more companies are pointing to AI when unveiling job cuts](https://apnews.com/article/65f9944fa25306bf5c975dd94805731e)

- **New Chicago Fed research says UBI is part of the serious policy conversation**
  A Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago working paper on forecasting AI’s economic effects finds that, under rapid-AI scenarios, experts see the labor force participation rate falling to 55% by 2050 and about 10 million jobs lost to AI. The same study says the public is more open than experts to broad responses, including Universal Basic Income, which makes UBI a live policy option rather than a fringe idea.  
  [Forecasting the Economic Effects of AI](https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/working-papers/2026/2026-07)

---

### What This Tells Us

The labor market is still functioning, but the signal is shifting from isolated AI anxiety to measurable restructuring pressure. BLS data will show whether turnover stays orderly, company announcements show AI being used to rationalize layoffs, and new research suggests policymakers should seriously consider income supports like UBI if displacement accelerates faster than job creation.
