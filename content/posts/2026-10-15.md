AI’s labor story today is less about one headline layoff and more about where employers are reshaping work, how governments are trying to limit risk, and who may be pushed toward weaker income protection if disruption arrives faster than adjustment.

---

### Key Stories

- **EU survey shows broader AI use in firms**
  The European Commission said on Oct. 14 that 42% of surveyed SMEs and mid-caps already use AI and algorithmic management, while 21% plan to adopt them later. That matters for labor displacement and UBI debates because it suggests AI is no longer confined to pilot projects; it is spreading into ordinary management decisions and job design.
  [Growing use of AI in European companies](https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/growing-use-ai-european-companies-2026-10-14_en?prefLang=lv)

- **ILO and World Bank flag uneven GenAI exposure**
  On Oct. 10, the ILO said a joint ILO–World Bank paper for the World Development Report 2026 examines GenAI exposure across 135 countries and warns that the digital divide shapes who benefits and who is vulnerable. The policy implication is direct: if lower-income economies face disruption before they can build connectivity, skills, and social protection, universal cash support becomes more than a theory.
  [New ILO–World Bank paper highlights uneven global impact of generative AI on jobs](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/new-ilo%E2%80%93world-bank-paper-highlights-uneven-global-impact-generative-ai-jobs)

- **DHS proposes new F-1 STEM OPT rules**
  The Federal Register on Oct. 8 published DHS’s proposed rule for F-1 STEM Optional Practical Training, including a fee and new compliance and recordkeeping requirements. DHS says the change could shift jobs toward U.S. workers and raise payroll-tax collections, making this a concrete policy move that could alter entry-level labor supply and the first rung of earnings security for foreign graduates.
  [DHS Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on F-1 STEM Optional Practical Training](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-08/html/2026-20660.htm)

### What This Tells Us

This week’s evidence points to a labor market adjustment problem, not just an automation technology story. Firms are adopting AI more broadly, international institutions are warning that the gains and losses will be uneven across countries, and governments are already changing workforce rules in ways that could reshape who gets access to jobs and income.
