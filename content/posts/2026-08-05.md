AI-driven restructuring is no longer a hypothetical threat to the labor market; it is showing up in layoffs, internal job reassignments, and legal fights over how companies decide who stays and who goes. At the same time, major institutions are warning that while broad displacement is not yet universal, the risks are real enough to justify stronger income-support and transition policies, including renewed debate over Universal Basic Income. ### Key Stories

- **Meta layoff lawsuit tests whether AI-assisted workforce cuts can discriminate**
  Twenty-six former Meta employees say the company used AI-powered tools to help select people for layoffs, including workers with disabilities or medical leave histories, and a federal judge has declined to block the cuts while the case proceeds. The dispute matters for labor because it shows how AI can be used not just to replace work, but to shape who absorbs the pain of restructuring.  
  [US judge won’t block Meta from laying off workers who filed AI discrimination lawsuit By Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/us-judge-wont-block-meta-from-laying-off-workers-who-filed-ai-discrimination-lawsuit-4798876)

- **Uber’s customer-service cuts show AI is moving from pilots into headcount reductions**
  Bloomberg reports that Uber cut 10% of its customer service jobs as part of an AI-driven shift, adding to evidence that automation is now being used to shrink support functions rather than merely assist them. For UBI advocates, this is the kind of concrete displacement pressure that strengthens the case for portable income support when routine service work gets restructured.  
  [Uber Cuts 10% of Customer Service Jobs to ‘Embrace’ AI (1)](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/tech-and-telecom-law/uber-cuts-10-of-customer-service-jobs-to-embrace-ai-1)

- **Google workers are demanding layoff protections as AI spending accelerates**
  More than 4,500 Google workers signed a petition calling for stronger protections against layoffs during the AI boom, a sign that employees themselves increasingly expect automation to be tied to job insecurity and internal redeployment. That kind of worker pressure suggests the policy debate is moving beyond “will AI create jobs?” to “who gets protected when it doesn’t?”  
  [Thousands of Google workers demand layoff protections amid AI boom in petition to CEO | Alphabet](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/jul/16/google-workers-layoff-protections-ai)

### What This Tells Us

The pattern is becoming harder to ignore: AI is reshaping labor in three ways at once — direct cuts, restructuring into new AI roles, and rising concern about fairness in how reductions are made. Official and institutional research still suggests we are not seeing uniform mass displacement, but the combination of company actions, employee backlash, and policy proposals from groups like the ILO points to a labor market under real transition pressure, which is exactly where UBI becomes more than an abstract idea.
