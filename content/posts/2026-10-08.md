AI is showing up less as a future threat than as an immediate workforce management issue: government is trying to raise worker readiness, while new research suggests adoption is already changing how jobs are filled and how firms organize work. The big question for income security is whether training can keep pace with restructuring if AI keeps spreading through existing occupations.

---

### Key Stories

- **DOL launches free AI literacy course**
  The Labor Department said on October 2 that workers can access its new "Make America AI-Ready" course by texting "READY" to 20202. For labor policy, that is a concrete sign the federal government is treating AI adjustment as a near-term workforce issue, not a distant one.
  [Department of Labor launches 'Make America AI-Ready' free AI literacy course](https://www.dol.gov/index.php/newsroom/releases/osec?page=0)

- **BEA finds AI use rising across firms and workers**
  BEA’s October 2 research spotlight says reported AI use climbed sharply, with worker use rising from roughly 20% in mid-2023 to nearly 50% by early 2026 and employer adoption moving from about 4% to 18%. The same study links higher AI utilization with stronger output and generally positive employment differences, suggesting the near-term risk is more job redesign and unequal gains than simple across-the-board layoffs.
  [AI Utilization and Changes in Economic Performance](https://apps.bea.gov/scb/spotlights/2026/1026-ai-utilization.htm)

- **Tracker shows AI changing jobs inside occupations**
  Revelio Labs’ October 1 tracker says 7.2% of U.S. job positions were held by workers with at least one reported AI skill, while the posting gap between the most and least AI-exposed occupations narrowed to -29%. That points to labor-market risk concentrated in changing task content and hiring patterns within occupations, which is exactly the kind of pressure that makes an income floor relevant if workers need time to retrain or move.
  [AI Labor Market Tracker: September 2026](https://www.reveliolabs.com/ai-labor-market-tracker/us/september-2026?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)

### What This Tells Us

Today’s evidence is less about a wave of layoffs than about a faster re-wiring of work itself: the federal government is pushing AI literacy, while both BEA and Revelio find AI spreading inside occupations and firms rather than only eliminating whole job categories. That combination suggests the most immediate policy gap is not just unemployment insurance after a layoff, but support for workers who are being pushed to adapt before the benefits of AI show up in paychecks.
