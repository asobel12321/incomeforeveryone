Public concern about AI is now as much about jobs and control as it is about the technology itself. A new AP-NORC poll finds most Americans think AI is moving too fast, while a White House science push underscores how aggressively Washington is still funding the next wave of tools.

---

### Key Stories

- **Most Americans want AI kept under human control**
  
  The AP-NORC poll shows 64% of Americans say AI is developing too fast, and most want government to prioritize worker protection and human oversight. For UBI readers, that matters because the political case for income support strengthens when public anxiety shifts from abstract innovation to concrete labor risk.
  
  [Most Americans think artificial intelligence is developing too fast, a new AP-NORC poll finds](https://apnews.com/article/9ce5531b7ea53234c4e600f8c0e9cf27)

- **White House launches more than $6 billion in science initiatives**
  
  The administration says the package is meant to expand AI/SI-enabled research capacity and support early-career STEM talent, showing policy still leans toward productivity gains rather than direct displacement relief. That leaves the workforce adjustment problem unresolved if automation accelerates faster than wages or retraining can absorb the shock.
  
  [Fact Sheet: Trump Administration Announces the Most Ambitious Set of Science Initiatives This Century](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/10/fact-sheet-trump-administration-announces-the-most-ambitious-set-of-science-initiatives-this-century/)

- **Silicon Valley pushes self-policing as safety disputes linger**
  
  AP reports AI founders are pressing for lighter-touch oversight even as the industry still faces internal safety fights, including recent firings at OpenAI. For labor policy, the key issue is governance: if firms can reorganize work quickly but regulate themselves loosely, displaced workers may need stronger automatic supports such as wage insurance or UBI.
  
  [In Silicon Valley, AI founders cheer Trump's calls for the industry to police itself on safety](https://apnews.com/article/32064fde8ad68c68f71d82336f7db525)

### What This Tells Us

Today’s evidence points to a widening gap between how fast AI is being deployed and how much public trust exists around its labor consequences. The policy response is still tilted toward accelerating capability and talent pipelines, while voters are signaling they want more protection for workers who may be caught in the middle.
