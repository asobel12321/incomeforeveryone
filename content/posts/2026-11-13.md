New U.S. evidence does not show a sudden wave of AI-triggered layoffs, but it does point to a more practical risk: workers in highly exposed jobs are finding it harder to get hired, especially younger workers. At the same time, social-protection systems are still being expanded to cushion displacement and earnings volatility rather than waiting for a full-blown crisis.

---

### Key Stories

- **AI exposure is hurting job seekers, not just incumbents**
  A Cleveland Fed commentary finds that AI-exposed occupations are associated with lower job-finding rates for unemployed workers, with younger workers hit more strongly; it says there is still little evidence of a broad rise in separations. That matters for UBI because the earliest labor-market damage may show up as longer job searches and weaker bargaining power before headline layoffs spike.
  [Job Finding and Unemployment in the Age of AI](https://www.clevelandfed.org/publications/economic-commentary/2026/ec-202626-job-finding-and-unemployment-in-the-age-of-ai)

- **The Census Bureau adds a fresh read on household strain**
  The Census Bureau released new Household Trends and Outlook Pulse Survey data on November 12 covering employment, housing costs, and mental health from about 136,000 households. For labor-policy watchers, that makes the current adjustment period easier to track: if AI and restructuring keep weighing on work, the knock-on effects on household finances and stress are likely to matter as much as the job totals themselves.
  [New Data Released From Household Trends and Outlook Pulse Survey](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/htops-nov12.html)

- **ILO-backed social protection work is widening the safety net**
  On November 8, Palestinian ministries launched a Social Protection Community of Practice with ILO support under an EU-funded project aimed at better coordination and broader access for women, youth, persons with disabilities, and informal workers. The relevance to UBI is direct: when labor markets are shakier and informal work is common, the policy debate shifts toward whether cash support can be made faster, broader, and less tied to stable payroll jobs.
  [Palestinian Ministries of Social Development and Labour launch new social protection platform with ILO support](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/palestinian-ministries-social-development-and-labour-launch-new-social)

### What This Tells Us

Today’s evidence suggests the AI labor story is becoming more specific: the first measurable harm may be weaker job-finding, not mass firings. That makes income support more relevant as a transition tool, because the pressure on workers can arrive quietly through longer unemployment spells, household strain, and uneven access to safety nets.
