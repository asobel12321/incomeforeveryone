New evidence suggests AI is moving deeper into the job market at the same time the labor market is losing momentum. The risk is not just job loss today, but a faster split between workers whose skills match the new demand and those who are left behind.

---

### Key Stories

- **Manufacturers are now asking for AI skills**
  A Federal Reserve note published September 30 finds AI-skill requirements in manufacturing postings surged in the second half of 2025, reaching 11% of postings versus 8% economy-wide. That matters for labor displacement because the pressure is shifting from abstract automation talk to concrete hiring requirements that can re-rank who gets work and who does not.
  [AI on the Factory Floor: Evidence from Manufacturing Job Postings](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/ai-on-the-factory-floor-evidence-from-manufacturing-job-postings-20260930.html)

- **Washington is funding training for AI-era work**
  On September 30, the Labor Department said it is awarding $43 million to seven states through its Industry-Driven Skills Training Fund, with priorities including advanced manufacturing, AI infrastructure, and IT involving AI. That is a direct policy response to the same restructuring pressure: if employers are changing the skill mix faster than workers can adapt, training becomes a front-line labor-market defense.
  [US Department of Labor awards $43 million to 7 states to support skills training in shipbuilding, manufacturing, aerospace, other critical industries](https://www.dol.gov/index.php/newsroom/releases?agency=39&state=All&topic=All&year=all)

- **Payroll growth stayed weak in September**
  The Labor Department reported on October 2 that U.S. payrolls rose by 29,000 in September and the unemployment rate held at 4.2%, while July and August payroll gains were revised down. For UBI debates, the important point is that AI-related restructuring is landing in a labor market that still has jobs, but less cushion for workers caught between slower hiring and changing skill demands.
  [The Employment Situation - September 2026](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/economicdata/empsit_10022026.pdf)

### What This Tells Us

Today’s evidence points to a labor shift that is more granular than mass layoffs: AI is increasingly appearing in job requirements, public policy is moving toward retraining, and overall payroll growth is soft enough that displaced workers may face longer searches before they can move. That combination strengthens the case for a more durable income backstop if restructuring accelerates faster than workers can be reskilled.
