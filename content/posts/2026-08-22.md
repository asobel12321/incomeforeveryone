AI is no longer just a productivity story; it is increasingly a workforce story. This week’s reporting shows companies trimming roles, refocusing hiring toward “AI-native” talent, and defending layoffs as part of broader restructuring while the labor market absorbs the transition.

---

### Key Stories

- **Amazon cuts jobs in its artificial intelligence group**
  Amazon confirmed cuts inside its artificial general intelligence organization, a signal that AI investment is not only creating new technical work but also eliminating overlapping roles inside the same buildout. For UBI advocates, that matters because displacement is starting to appear even in the teams building the automation itself.  
  [Amazon cuts jobs in its artificial general intelligence group](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/amazon-cuts-jobs-in-its-artificial-general-intelligence-group-4806431)

- **Thomson Reuters plans up to 500 engineering layoffs while hiring AI-native staff**
  Thomson Reuters said it will eliminate up to 500 jobs, or about 1.8% of its workforce, while also planning to hire more than 250 new engineering roles over the next two years, mostly senior and AI-native. That is a classic restructuring pattern: fewer traditional roles, more specialized AI-centric roles, and a narrower path for mid-career workers to stay attached to the labor market.  
  [Thomson Reuters to cut ’small number’ of engineering jobs](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/thomson-reuters-to-cut-small-number-of-engineering-jobs-4789191)

- **Cisco joins the wave of AI-era cuts as tech firms keep slimming headcount**
  AP reported Cisco’s plan to cut under 4,000 jobs, even as it posted record revenue on strong AI-related demand. The labor-market lesson is uncomfortable: firms can boost AI spending, report growth, and still reduce payrolls, which strengthens the case for income support if restructuring continues to outrun new job creation.  
  [Streamline operations: How AI is fueling tech world layoffs and job cuts](https://apnews.com/article/ai-layoffs-cisco-meta-block-65f9944fa25306bf5c975dd94805731e)

---

### What This Tells Us

The pattern is becoming clearer: AI is not just automating tasks, it is changing how companies justify headcount decisions. Even where overall labor-market damage is still contained, the combination of restructuring, selective hiring, and role compression suggests more workers will face unstable transitions — exactly the kind of friction that makes Universal Basic Income harder to dismiss as a fringe idea and easier to view as contingency planning.
