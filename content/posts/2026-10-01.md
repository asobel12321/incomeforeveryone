California just moved to curb AI’s role in workplace surveillance and mass layoffs, even as the broader U.S. labor market remains relatively stable and layoffs stay low. That tension captures the moment: companies are restructuring for automation, while policymakers and workers are still arguing over who should absorb the shock.

---

### Key Stories

- **California tightens worker protections around AI-driven layoffs**
  California Gov. Gavin Newsom signed laws requiring employers to notify workers when AI is responsible for mass layoffs and banning AI-based firing decisions and biometric emotional surveillance. The move is a direct response to labor-market risk from automation, and it strengthens the case for a larger backstop such as UBI if AI-driven restructuring accelerates.  
  [California Gov. Gavin Newsom signs laws to protect workers from AI risks](https://apnews.com/article/1aa4935e3ee79519ae8b2458b2b9ebc1)

- **U.S. layoffs remain low, but job openings fell in August**
  Reuters reported that U.S. job openings declined in August while layoffs stayed low, suggesting employers are still cautious about broad workforce cuts even as demand cools. For UBI advocates, that kind of “quiet” labor market can mask a weaker picture for workers if hiring slows while firms keep automating and reorganizing.  
  [US job openings fall in August; layoffs remain low](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/us-job-openings-fall-august-layoffs-remain-low-2026-09-29/)

- **AI-linked restructuring continues at major companies**
  Reuters reported on Oracle’s additional restructuring costs as it ramps up AI spending, underscoring how companies are pairing automation investment with workforce changes. This is exactly the kind of pattern that makes income guarantees relevant: productivity gains can coexist with fewer roles, especially in middle management and routine knowledge work.  
  [Oracle to spend $700 million more on restructuring costs as it ramps up AI spending](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/oracle-spend-700-million-more-restructuring-costs-it-ramps-up-ai-spending-2026-09-19/)

---

### What This Tells Us

The labor market is not collapsing, but the adjustment is becoming more structural: firms are using AI to justify restructuring, lawmakers are starting to regulate those changes, and workers are being asked to absorb the uncertainty. That is the core UBI argument in 2026—if automation keeps shifting bargaining power away from labor, income support may need to become part of the workforce transition, not an afterthought.
