AI is reshaping the labor conversation in two directions at once: headline layoffs tied to restructuring and automation are intensifying, while official labor-market data still show historically low layoffs overall. That tension is exactly why UBI and broader income supports remain central to the policy debate.

---

### Key Stories

- **California forces transparency on AI-linked layoffs**
  California Gov. Gavin Newsom signed measures requiring employers to notify workers if AI is responsible for mass layoffs and barring AI from being used to decide firings, a notable signal that regulators are treating automation as a workforce issue, not just an efficiency tool. For UBI advocates, this is a reminder that even when companies say “restructuring,” the human cost can be large enough to warrant explicit public disclosure and stronger transition policy.  
  [California Gov. Gavin Newsom signs laws to protect workers from AI risks](https://apnews.com/article/1aa4935e3ee79519ae8b2458b2b9ebc1)

- **Official labor data still show low layoffs, even as job creation cools**
  The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that September payrolls rose only modestly and unemployment stayed at 4.2%, while AP’s read of the latest claims data said layoffs remain low. That combination suggests the labor market is not in a broad collapse yet, but it also leaves workers vulnerable if AI-driven restructuring accelerates before new jobs are created.  
  [The Employment Situation — September 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm)

- **Large company cuts continue to be framed as restructuring, but AI is in the background**
  Reuters reported earlier this year that Uber planned to cut about 3,300 jobs, or 10% of its workforce, as it reduced management layers and redirected spending toward ride-sharing, delivery, and robotaxi efforts. Even when firms do not explicitly blame AI for the layoffs, the shift toward automation-adjacent business models shows how technology can drive workforce restructuring without a single “AI layoff” headline.  
  [Uber to lay off 10% of staff in biggest cuts since COVID](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/uber-to-lay-off-10-of-staff-in-biggest-cuts-since-covid-4887918)

### What This Tells Us

The pattern is becoming clearer: companies may use restructuring language, but automation and AI are increasingly part of the economic logic behind job cuts. With the broader labor market still relatively stable, the policy question is shifting from crisis response to prevention — how to build income supports, retraining, and possibly UBI before the next wave of displacement hits.
