AI-driven restructuring is no longer a theoretical risk for the labor market; it is showing up in real headcount cuts, especially in tech and knowledge-work roles. At the same time, policymakers and researchers are increasingly debating whether income supports such as Universal Basic Income will be needed to cushion the transition.

---

### Key Stories

- **Microsoft trims 4,800 jobs as it shifts more resources toward AI**
  Microsoft said it is cutting about 2.1% of its workforce, or roughly 4,800 jobs, while restructuring parts of its commercial and Xbox businesses and spending heavily on AI infrastructure. For labor advocates, the key issue is that even when companies frame cuts as restructuring, AI investment can still accelerate job displacement and weaken bargaining power across the broader workforce.  
  [Microsoft joins AI-driven tech layoff wave with 4,800 job cuts](https://www.reuters.com/business/world-at-work/microsoft-joins-ai-driven-tech-layoff-wave-with-4800-job-cuts-2026-07-06/)

- **Amazon cuts jobs in its AI group, showing that even AI builders are shedding labor**
  Amazon said it cut jobs in its artificial general intelligence group, adding to a series of smaller reductions across the company this year. That matters for workforce planning because it suggests AI is not only changing frontline work; it is also reshaping hiring and staffing inside the teams building the technology itself.  
  [Amazon cuts jobs in its artificial general intelligence group](https://www.reuters.com/business/world-at-work/amazon-cuts-jobs-in-its-artificial-general-intelligence-group-2026-07-22/)

- **Thomson Reuters cuts engineering roles while expanding AI-native hiring**
  Thomson Reuters said it is cutting a small number of engineering jobs as it deploys AI more aggressively, while also planning to hire more than 250 net-new engineering roles over the next two years, mostly senior and AI-native. That split is a preview of the labor-market reordering ahead: fewer routine technical roles, more demand for workers who can supervise, integrate, and adapt AI systems.  
  [Thomson Reuters to cut 'small number' of engineering jobs](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/thomson-reuters-cut-small-number-engineering-jobs-2026-07-13/)

---

### What This Tells Us

The latest wave of layoffs suggests AI is increasingly being used alongside broader restructuring, not always as the sole cause, but often as a catalyst for reorganizing work and shrinking teams. That makes the UBI question more urgent: if displacement continues while the gains from automation accrue unevenly, income support and transition policy may need to become part of mainstream labor-market planning.
