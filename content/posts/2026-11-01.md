The freshest labor signal today is not a mass-layoff headline but a policy-and-practice shift: the UK’s 2026 AI adoption survey shows more firms using AI, while government is also rolling out an AI-enabled Work Hub and the ILO is training support officers to use AI for migrant livelihoods. Together, those moves point to a labor market adapting around automation in real time, not just reacting after the fact.

---

### Key Stories

- **UK survey shows AI adoption rising**
  The UK government’s 2026 survey gives a current snapshot of how businesses are actually deploying AI, and it matters because adoption is the channel through which future job redesign, displacement, and retraining pressure tends to spread. If firms are scaling AI to improve efficiency, the labor question shifts from “whether” automation arrives to which tasks and workers get reorganized first.
  [AI adoption survey 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-adoption-survey-2026)

- **DWP launches AI-enabled Work Hub**
  The Department for Work and Pensions’ new Work Hub is a concrete example of public agencies using AI to steer people toward jobs, training, and career pathways. That is relevant to income security because it shows governments preparing support systems for workers facing more churn and more fragmented job matches.
  [DWP: Work Hub](https://www.gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/dwp-work-hub)

- **ILO trains support staff on AI for livelihoods**
  The ILO says it held an AI training in Manila from 23–25 October 2026 to help reintegration officers and partners support Filipino migrant entrepreneurs. The immediate UBI relevance is indirect but real: when workers need faster pathways back into income, AI is increasingly being positioned as a tool for casework, matching, and small-business support rather than only a source of disruption.
  [AI training to strengthen support to Filipino migrant entrepreneurs](https://www.ilo.org/meetings-and-events/ai-training-strengthen-support-filipino-migrant-entrepreneurs)

### What This Tells Us

Today’s evidence points to a more practical phase of the AI economy: firms are adopting, governments are deploying support tools, and labor institutions are experimenting with AI to keep people attached to work and income. That does not prove large layoffs are imminent everywhere, but it does show the infrastructure for workforce reshaping is being built now, which is exactly why UBI and other income-stabilization policies keep coming back into the conversation.
