The freshest labor-market signal today is not a layoff wave but a policy response: governments are tightening job-market rules and expanding training and social protection as AI and automation reshape work. The new evidence shows that adjustment is being managed through transparency, skills, and coverage reforms rather than through crisis measures alone.

---

### Key Stories

- **UK plans mandatory pay disclosure in job ads**
  The UK government says it will introduce a “say what you pay” rule, requiring employers to publish salary information in job adverts. For labor-market risk, the concrete change is that jobseekers—especially younger applicants—would have clearer bargaining information at a time when AI and automation may be narrowing entry-level openings.
  [New ‘say what you pay’ law to help young people into work](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-say-what-you-pay-law-to-help-young-people-into-work)

- **ILO says AI’s labor effects will be gradual**
  The ILO’s new brief argues that AI will reshape jobs in developing economies unevenly, with outcomes depending on policy choices, infrastructure, skills, and social protection. The relevance for UBI debates is direct: the report treats income security and adaptation policy as central if workers are to absorb technological change without falling behind.
  [AI and developing economies: Promoting decent work in a time of rapid technological change](https://www.ilo.org/publications/ai-and-developing-economies-promoting-decent-work-time-rapid-technological)

- **EU links vocational training to technology change**
  The European Commission launched a new vocational skills strategy that uses business partnerships, flexible training modules, and new training centers to speed workforce adjustment. The concrete development is a fresh EU policy package aimed at helping workers move into jobs shaped by digitalization and AI rather than waiting for displacement to deepen.
  [Commission launches strategy to strengthen vocational skills across Europe](https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-launches-strategy-strengthen-vocational-skills-across-europe-2026-11-05_en?prefLang=el)

### What This Tells Us

Today’s evidence points to a more practical phase of the AI-and-labor debate: policymakers are focusing less on predicting a single job-killing shock and more on reducing the damage from uneven adjustment. Pay transparency, workforce training, and broader social insurance are emerging as the main tools for keeping workers connected to opportunity as automation pressures the labor market.
