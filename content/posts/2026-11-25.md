The newest evidence today points to a labor market that is adapting around AI before any single displacement shock becomes visible. In the EU, policymakers are linking slower job creation to skills policy, while employers are increasingly treating AI as both a training issue and a workforce-risk issue.

---

### Key Stories

- **EU links slower job creation to AI skills push**
  The European Commission says the EU labor market remains resilient, but job creation has slowed enough that it is proposing a human-capital recommendation focused on AI literacy, skills shortages, and moving workers out of declining sectors. For UBI watchers, the key signal is not mass layoffs but a policy shift toward transition support as AI and digital tools change which workers can move and who gets left behind.
  [Commission publishes proposal for Joint Employment Report 2027 and pushes Recommendation on Human Capital](https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-publishes-proposal-joint-employment-report-2027-and-pushes-recommendation-human-capital-2026-11-24_en?prefLang=el)

- **HR leaders say AI training is now widespread**
  WorldatWork reports that 82% of surveyed HR leaders say their organizations use AI in HR processes, and 80% say their workforce has received AI training. The same survey says AI’s workforce impact is now their top organizational concern, which suggests employers are trying to manage adoption and displacement risk at the same time.
  [WorldatWork reports HR leaders say AI training is widespread and workforce impact is now a top concern](https://worldatwork.org/publications/worldatwork-today/how-hr-leaders-are-navigating-ai-adoption-economics-and-skills-gaps)

- **Employers warn AI is worsening hiring fraud risk**
  WorldatWork says AI is making resume fraud, synthetic identities, and hiring deception easier to produce and harder to catch, pushing employers toward tighter screening and verification. That matters for labor markets because more automation in recruiting can create a second-order effect: fewer trusted signals for who is genuinely qualified, and more pressure on workers to prove identity and skills.
  [WorldatWork warns AI is speeding up employment fraud and legal risk](https://worldatwork.org/publications/worldatwork-today/mitigating-employment-based-fraud-and-legal-risks-in-the-age-of-ai)

### What This Tells Us

Today's evidence is less about immediate AI layoffs than about institutional preparation for churn. Policymakers are prioritizing skills portability, and employers are responding with training and tighter screening, which means the labor-market risk is shifting toward slower transitions, more gatekeeping, and greater pressure on support systems if displaced workers cannot move quickly enough.
