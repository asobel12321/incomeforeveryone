AI is no longer just a future labor-market risk; it is now showing up in company layoff notices, restructuring memos, and broader signs of a cooling job market. Today’s evidence points to a pattern UBI advocates have long warned about: firms capture the gains from automation faster than workers can absorb the losses.

---

### Key Stories

- **AI-linked layoffs continue from big tech to information services**
  Reuters reporting in July showed Microsoft, Amazon, and Thomson Reuters all cutting jobs while shifting more resources toward AI infrastructure and AI-native roles. The labor-policy relevance is straightforward: even when companies say “restructuring,” the direction of travel is fewer routine roles and more pressure on workers to retrain quickly.  
  [Factbox-Companies cutting jobs as investments shift toward AI](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/factboxcompanies-cutting-jobs-as-investments-shift-toward-ai-4776976)

- **Etsy cuts 12% of its workforce in a restructuring plan tied to faster execution and changing work**
  Reuters reported on August 5 that Etsy is laying off about 220 employees as part of a restructuring aimed at improving coordination and speed, while also acknowledging that AI is changing how products are built and problems are solved. For UBI, this matters because it shows how AI is blending into broader workforce restructuring, making displacement harder to separate from “efficiency.”  
  [Etsy lays off 12% of workforce as part of restructuring plan](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/etsy-lays-off-12-worker-200744617.html)

- **The labor market is softening even before AI’s full effects are visible**
  Reuters reported on August 7 that the U.S. unexpectedly lost jobs in July and that earlier months were revised lower, raising fresh concerns about job-market resilience. That backdrop strengthens the case for income supports like UBI, because a weaker labor market reduces workers’ bargaining power just as AI-related restructuring accelerates.  
  [US nonfarm payrolls unexpectedly decline in July; unemployment rate eases to 4.1%](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/us-nonfarm-payrolls-fall-july-124023322.html/)

---

### What This Tells Us

The clearest pattern right now is not a single AI “job apocalypse,” but a steady shift toward fewer middle-skill and routine white-collar jobs, more selective hiring, and more pressure on workers to absorb disruption on their own. If that trend continues, UBI becomes less of a thought experiment and more of a practical stabilizer for households facing repeated layoffs, shorter job ladders, and weaker wage growth.
