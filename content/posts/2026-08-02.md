AI is no longer just a product story; it is increasingly a workforce story. Companies are using automation, restructuring, and AI investment to justify layoffs and reorganizations, while public data and research continue to point to uneven labor-market risk and growing pressure for income supports like Universal Basic Income.

---

### Key Stories

- **Meta employees allege AI helped select layoff targets**
  A group of 26 Meta workers sued the company, claiming internal AI systems and productivity data were used to pick employees for layoffs, including people on medical and parental leave. The case underscores a core UBI argument: when firms automate management decisions as well as tasks, workers can face abrupt income shocks with little procedural protection.  
  [26 Meta employees sue, alleging AI-driven layoff picks hit workers on medical and parental leave](https://apnews.com/article/019fb9c7fdc09167e91547546bce5be8)

- **Amazon said generative AI will reduce its corporate workforce**
  Amazon’s January layoff announcement said the company was cutting about 16,000 corporate jobs while also anticipating generative AI would reduce corporate headcount in the coming years. That combination of restructuring plus automation investment is the clearest example of how AI is being used to rationalize leaner staffing—and why basic income advocates see a need for a more durable safety net.  
  [Amazon cuts about 16,000 corporate jobs in the latest round of layoffs](https://apnews.com/article/74387fae2313ff7b0b1e638c00863443)

- **BLS says the next worker-displacement release is coming August 27**
  The Bureau of Labor Statistics has scheduled its next Worker Displacement release for August 27, 2026, alongside new Employment Projections and Occupational Outlook data. That timing matters because it will give policymakers a fresh, official read on how much churn workers are facing just as firms accelerate AI-enabled restructuring and job redesign.  
  [Schedule of Selected Releases for August 2026](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/2026/08_sched.htm)

---

### What This Tells Us

The pattern is becoming harder to miss: AI is being used both as a direct rationale for layoffs and as part of broader corporate restructuring that trims payrolls, flattens organizations, and shifts work toward fewer, higher-skill roles. If displacement keeps outrunning job creation, Universal Basic Income will look less like an abstract policy experiment and more like a practical response to rising labor-market volatility.
