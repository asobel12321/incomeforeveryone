AI is no longer just a productivity story; it is increasingly a labor story. In July, major firms announced more job cuts tied to AI investment and restructuring, while economists warned that policymakers are moving too slowly on the risk of large-scale displacement.

---

### Key Stories

- **Microsoft’s AI pivot adds to 2026 layoff wave**
  Reuters reported that Microsoft cut about 2.1% of its workforce, or roughly 4,800 jobs, as it reshapes parts of its business while shifting more investment toward AI infrastructure. For labor markets, this is a sign that automation spending can coincide with headcount reduction even at highly profitable firms — the kind of dynamic that strengthens the case for income supports like UBI.  
  [Companies cutting jobs as investments shift toward AI](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/companies-cutting-jobs-as-investments-shift-toward-ai-2025-07-06/)

- **Thomson Reuters trims engineering roles while expanding AI-first hiring**
  Reuters reported that Thomson Reuters is cutting a “small number” of engineering jobs as it deploys AI more aggressively, while also planning to add more AI-native engineers over time. That mix — fewer traditional roles, more specialized AI roles — is exactly the kind of workforce restructuring that can leave mid-career workers exposed and pushes the UBI debate from theory toward contingency planning.  
  [Thomson Reuters to cut 'small number' of engineering jobs](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/thomson-reuters-cut-small-number-engineering-jobs-2026-07-13/)

- **Economists warn governments to act now on AI job displacement**
  The Associated Press reported that hundreds of economists signed an open letter urging institutions to respond quickly to AI’s economic impact, including the risk of large-scale job displacement. For UBI advocates, this matters because the policy conversation is shifting from “if” AI disrupts work to “how” society will absorb the income shock when it does.  
  [Hundreds of economists say 'we must act now' on AI’s economic impact and job displacement risks](https://apnews.com/article/783469467e0df1463df44518f33295ee)

---

### What This Tells Us

The pattern is becoming clearer: AI investment is not just creating new tools, it is reshaping payrolls. When companies cut staff while reorienting around automation and economists openly warn that displacement could scale quickly, the policy question becomes less about whether disruption is coming and more about what stabilizers — wage insurance, retraining, and possibly UBI — are ready before the damage spreads.
