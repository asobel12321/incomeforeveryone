New ILO reporting this week did not bring a layoff headline, but it did sharpen the policy response: the agency is framing social protection as a core tool for workers facing automation, platformization, and unstable earnings. The clearest update is that the conversation is moving from abstract AI risk to practical income-security design for workers already exposed to disruption.

---

### Key Stories

- **ILO urges stronger universal social protection**
  The ILO’s November 6 conference report says governments should renew action on universal social protection and connect cash support more directly to decent-work pathways. For AI and automation, that matters because the adjustment problem is not just lost jobs but the income gap workers face while they retrain, search, or move between roles.
  [Global conference calls for renewed action on universal social protection](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/global-conference-calls-renewed-action-universal-social-protection)

- **Jordan expands coverage for own-account workers**
  On November 5, the ILO announced a partnership to expand social security protection for own-account workers in Jordan. That is relevant to automation risk because independent workers are often the least buffered when labor demand shifts, making access to protection against income loss and old-age insecurity more important.
  [Jordan launches partnership to expand social security protection for own-account workers](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/jordan-launches-partnership-expand-social-security-protection-own-account)

- **Nepal’s transport work remains largely informal**
  The ILO’s November 5 study found nearly three in four transport jobs in Nepal are informal even though business registration is widespread, with ride-hailing and delivery work offering income but limited protections. This is the kind of labor-market structure that leaves workers especially vulnerable if automation, apps, or restructuring squeeze earnings without compensation.
  [Nearly three in four transport jobs in Nepal are informal despite widespread business registration, ILO finds](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/nearly-three-four-transport-jobs-nepal-are-informal-despite-widespread)

### What This Tells Us

Today’s evidence points to a narrower but more actionable UBI debate: the pressing issue is not only whether AI displaces jobs, but whether workers can survive the transition when work becomes less stable, more contingent, and less protected. The ILO’s focus this week suggests that income support, not just reskilling, is increasingly central to how governments may soften automation shocks.
