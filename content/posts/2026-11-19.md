AI is showing up this week not as a headline mass-layoff event, but as a rules-and-pay issue: the ILO has put two fresh releases on the table that connect AI directly to remuneration, algorithmic management, and income security. A separate official labour-market update from Northern Ireland adds a timely snapshot of payrolled employment and wages as workers face a softer, more automated economy.

---

### Key Stories

- **ILO warns AI can reshape pay in media**
  The ILO’s November 17 article says AI and streaming are changing remuneration, working time, skills, and income security in media and culture, including through systems that can replicate voices, images, and performances. For UBI debates, the key point is that displacement risk is not only about job loss; it is also about weaker bargaining power and thinner earnings in creative work.
  [Who shares the value? AI, streaming and decent work in media and culture](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/who-shares-value-ai-streaming-and-decent-work-media-and-culture)

- **ILO pushes human review for platform deactivations**
  In another November 17 release, the ILO said its new Convention on Decent Work in the Platform Economy addresses automated systems, algorithmic task allocation, monitoring, transparency, and human review of deactivation decisions. That is a direct response to workforce restructuring by software, where workers can lose access to income without the protections usually tied to layoffs.
  [Wages and social dialogue are key levers for improving job quality](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/wages-and-social-dialogue-are-key-levers-improving-job-quality)

- **Northern Ireland posts 822,800 payrolled employees**
  Northern Ireland’s November 17 labour market release reports 822,800 payrolled employees in October 2026 and a median monthly pay of £2,525. That makes this a useful baseline for assessing how much labor-market strain AI-driven restructuring might add if hiring slows or earnings growth weakens further.
  [Labour Market statistics - November 2026](https://www.economy-ni.gov.uk/news/labour-market-statistics-november-2026)

---

### What This Tells Us

Today’s evidence points to a more specific AI labor problem than a simple layoff wave: institutions are increasingly worried about how automation changes pay, monitoring, and access to work itself. That makes income support relevant not just when workers are dismissed, but when AI weakens earnings, stability, and the ability to contest unfair offboarding or opaque task allocation.
